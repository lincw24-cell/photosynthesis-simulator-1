Rate response

The curve shows the predicted rate at the current RuBP setting as light intensity changes. The dot marks your current settings.

Light reactions provide ATP and NADPH; RuBP accepts CO₂ in the Calvin cycle.

How to interpret the model

  • Increase sunlight: the rate rises when light is limiting, then approaches a plateau as RuBP availability becomes the bottleneck.
  • Increase RuBP: more CO₂-acceptor substrate is available, so the modeled Calvin-cycle rate can rise—especially when light supplies enough energy.
  • Try a fair comparison: change one slider at a time and note the rate. At zero light, the model predicts essentially no sustained photosynthetic rate even if RuBP is abundant.

Biology note: RuBP is regenerated during the Calvin cycle. This slider represents its effective availability, not a claim that plants can freely accumulate unlimited RuBP. Real photosynthesis depends on several interacting limiting factors.
